## Landing scene (prototype v2)
- Scene: giant white glowing sculpture (`pintura final.glb`), ~15 m tall, buried to the chest; starting view is 3/4, face turned to frame-right. [Matches Juan's renders]
- Two modes, toggle (Sun) / (Moon); default follows visitor's local time (night 19:00–06:00). `#day` / `#night` in the URL force a mode.
- Day: vibrant blue sky, soft wispy clouds (the first procedural version; volumetric clouds were rejected as fake-looking), visible sun disc, chrome dragonflies that dart toward the cursor at the depth you point.
- Night: **after-sunset / blue hour** (indigo zenith → dusty rose → amber horizon glow behind the sculpture, cool fill, warm rim light), waning crescent moon (sun disc morphs into it), fireflies that follow the cursor. Purple dusk kept as alternative in `v2-alt-purple-night.html`.
- Camera: very slow, subtle automatic drift; drag to orbit; gentle eased parallax. [Reduce motion]
- Grass: dense interactive field around the sculpture (wind + cursor parting), extra-dense strip near the camera, distant blades blend to turf tone to avoid sparkle.
- *(Replaced: see "grass runs through" below.)* Grass meets the sculpture with the original soft cut-off. Precise footprint masks / stencil cut-outs were tried and rejected: they made the sculpture look pasted on.
- Distant hills: **live grass all the way up**. The valley was reshaped so the hills rise 16–44 m from the sculpture and their crest hides everything beyond; a second, world-fixed grass layer covers the hills, thinning with distance while blades widen so on-screen density matches the field. Same wind, colour and lighting as the field. (Replaced the baked panorama, which looked different from up close; kept in `v2-alt-baked-hills.html`.)
- Mode toggle is icon-only (sun / crescent), no "(Sun)" "(Moon)" words; labels kept for screen readers via aria-label. [Icons are enough]
- Third mode **Ocean** (wave icon): the field becomes open ocean at sunrise. Low warm sun on the horizon with a glitter path toward the camera, peach/rose/violet sky, glowing reflection of the sculpture, foam at its waterline, cursor ripples, golden water droplets instead of dragonflies. Land ↔ ocean switches with a short fade; `#ocean` forces it. Pre-ocean version kept in `v2-alt-before-ocean.html`.
- Ocean refinements: the sculpture glows brighter at sunrise (stronger emission, lower bloom threshold). Particles are **floating water beads**: ~40 small wobbling spheres (4–13 cm), highly reflective (sky, sun glints, the sculpture's glow) with a clear, slightly refracted core. They drift slowly like the fireflies and all of them ease toward the cursor (spread in loose layers so they don't clump). Earlier versions kept: tiny glow droplets `v2-alt-ocean-droplets.html`, big blobs `v2-alt-ocean-big-blobs.html` (too big, moved too much).
- Dragonflies, fireflies and water beads follow the cursor everywhere: over ground/sea they go to the point you hover; over the sky they rise to a point ~16 m along your line of sight. Grass parting and water ripples only react when the cursor is over the ground or sea.
- Sculpture glow is identical in every mode (emission and bloom set relative to each mode's exposure). Hover: it glows brighter and lights its surroundings more, cursor becomes a pointer. Click (not drag): a bright pulse, then the About overlay opens.
- Same camera framing on every screen size (the mobile framing: 15 m out, 1.0 rad view), so resizing no longer zooms in.
- About = **face focus**: clicking the sculpture (or (About)) pulses the glow, then the camera swoops in on a slight arc and orbit to a **true side profile** of the head, face looking toward the text (3/4 version kept in `v2-alt-about-three-quarter.html`). Desktop: head on the left, text on the right. Mobile/portrait: head low, text above it. The interface fades out, the glow softens so the face reads; (Close), Esc or clicking the scene flies back. `#about` opens it directly. Pre-focus version kept in `v2-alt-before-face-focus.html`.
- Grass layers hand off by thinning (blades drop out gradually) instead of shrinking, and the ground uses smooth per-vertex normals. [Removed the ring/stripe lines across the field]
- UI glass is drawn by the renderer itself (not CSS backdrop-filter or a library): the mode toggle, nav and About panel get a frosted blur of the live scene, lens-like refraction and a slight colour fringe at the edges, a specular rim lit from top-left and a soft shadow. Positions are read from the DOM every frame, so CSS animations still drive it. Light glass by day/ocean, dark glass at night. [Works the same in Chrome, Safari and Firefox; CSS-filter libraries only refract in Chromium.] CSS-glass version kept in `v2-alt-css-glass.html`.
- **Mobile orbit = the phone itself.** On touch devices, dragging no longer rotates the environment (touch still steers the dragonflies/fireflies/beads). Physically turning the phone orbits the scene instead, via the device-orientation sensor, smoothed and calibrated to wherever the phone points on load. iOS asks for motion permission on the first tap; without permission (or with reduced motion) the scene simply stays put. Mouse drag on desktop is unchanged. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-mobile-gyro.html`. [Needs a real-phone test: direction and strength (`GYRO_GAIN`) are a first guess]
- **New sculpture: the open mind** (`portfolio 3d2.blend`). The body's skull is cut open and two nested inner heads sit inside it, a symbol of ideas and the subconscious. Same glowing material, same three environments. The new body is fitted onto the old one (5 cm median error), so the grass footprint, hover area, ocean reflection and About face-focus framing are unchanged. Extracted with `tools/extract_mind_figure.py` → `assets/data/figure-mind.json`. Previous sculpture kept in `v2-alt-before-mind-sculpture.html`.
- **Hover = ideas surfacing.** Hovering the sculpture still brightens it, and now the two inner heads also rise out of the open skull. The outer head goes first (32 cm), then the inner one follows it higher (64 cm) with a soft spring overshoot and a slow float while held. They sink back when the cursor leaves. With reduced motion they ease up and down without bounce or float. Touch has no hover, so phones don't see it yet.
- Inside the open skull, surfaces facing away from the viewer render at about 55% brightness, so the cut reads as a hollow rather than a flat white cap.
- **Heads already apart at rest.** Before any hover, the outer head sits 12 cm and the inner one 24 cm up out of the open skull, so the layers read as a stepped stack. Hover lifts them to the same heights as before (32 / 64 cm). Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-rest-lift.html`.
- Camera raised a little: the eye is about 1.9 m above the water (was 1.05 m), with the same aim point and distance on every screen. A much higher camera (about 4.6 m) was rejected; see below.
- **Desktop camera closer:** 12 m from the sculpture (phones stay at 15 m), so it reads bigger on large screens. This replaces "same framing on every screen size" for desktop.
- **Grass runs through the sculpture.** The oval cut-out around the body is removed, so the field continues right up to and into it, and the body hides the blades inside. The oval never matched the body's shape, so orbiting revealed a bare ring of ground. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-grass-through.html`.
- **Grass stops at the sculpture's true footprint.** The body is sliced at the heights the grass occupies (0.25–0.95 m) and the slices are merged into one outline, the torso plus arms, about 4.5 × 3.6 m. Blades stop exactly at that outline (5 cm precision), with no oval and no ring. This works where the earlier footprint masks didn't, because there is no gap or clearing: the field touches the body. Built with `tools/footprint_mind.py` → `assets/data/figure-footprint-mind.json`. Previous version (grass running through the body) kept in `v2-alt-before-exact-footprint.html`.
- **Calmer dragonflies, a bigger swarm on hover.** Wing beat is unchanged, but the flight is calmer. They pick a new spot every 1–2 s while following the cursor (was 0.35–1.2 s) and every 2.5–5 s while wandering. They glide there on a soft, fully damped spring with no stop-and-go overshoot, turn about 3× more slowly, and top out at 4.5 m/s. Nine wander at rest (six on phones). When the cursor moves, nine more (four on phones) glide down from just above the frame and join the swarm within about 2 s, then drift back up out of view when the cursor goes quiet. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-calm-dragonflies.html`.
- **No colour ring in the grass.** The visible arc behind the sculpture was the hand-off about 14 m out between the main field and the hill grass, which shaded blades differently with distance. Both now use the same distance tone. (The sculpture footprint was already correct; it wasn't the cause.) Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-grass-ring.html`.
- **One follow speed for every swarm.** Dragonflies, fireflies and water beads all close in on the cursor or finger at the same rate (`FOLLOW`), arriving together in about 1.5 s. Before, the dragonflies only re-aimed every 1–2 s and the beads moved at half speed, so their follow was hard to see. Dragonflies now track the cursor every frame and drift around it; when wandering they keep the calm glide. More of them join on hover: 19 more on desktop (28 in all), 10 more on phones (16 in all). Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-unified-follow.html`.
- **Phone rotation constrained.** Turning the phone still orbits the scene horizontally only, but less: about half the phone's turn, capped at about ±20° with a soft stop. Sudden sensor jumps from tipping the phone hard are absorbed instead of spinning the scene, and the view eases back to the front over about 4 s, so turning your whole body doesn't leave it stuck at the edge. Tuning: `GYRO_GAIN`, `GYRO_MAX`, `GYRO_RECENTER`. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-gyro-limit.html`. [Still needs a real-phone test]
- **Ocean = a true mirror.** The sculpture's reflection used to be faked with three hand-placed ovals sized for the old figure, so it came out wider or narrower than the body depending on the angle. Now the sculpture and the rocks are rendered upside-down below the waterline each frame (clipped at the surface, full resolution) and the water shows that image, rippled by the waves and slightly darkened like real water. The water is also more mirror-like overall. The oval foam ring and the glow pool on the water around the sculpture are removed. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-real-reflection.html`.
- **Soft, non-literal reflection.** The mirrored image is defocused (blurred, with the blur stretched vertically like light smeared on moving water), about 30% darker and slightly see-through, so the water suggests the sculpture instead of copying it.
- **Glass blobs instead of water beads.** The floating spheres are now rigid, clear glass: a slightly irregular hand-blown shape that turns slowly, refraction that flips the world inside them like a glass ball (sea on top, sky below), a faint rainbow fringe at the edges, crisp sun glints and a soft rim. Motion and cursor-following are unchanged. Previous version (sharp mirror, water beads) kept in `v2-alt-before-soft-reflection-glass.html`.
- **Rocks rebuilt as natural islands.** Your rock shapes are now scaled up evenly (3× wider, 2.6× taller) instead of only stretched upward, and smoothed so the peaks are rounder. They're grouped into loose islands with open water between them, from about 55–70 m out to the horizon. The gap toward the sun is gone; the layout was chosen so the opening view has islands on both sides of the sun. Tuning: `ROCK_SCALE`, `ROCK_VSCALE`. Previous version (spiky wall, sun gap) kept in `v2-alt-before-natural-rocks.html`.
- **Clean grass edge on phones.** Phones get less than half the blades, slightly wider, so single tall blades used to stand out and tilt over the base of the body. Now blades shorten over the last few centimetres before the footprint (18 cm on desktop, 45 cm on phones), and wind doesn't tilt them right next to the body. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-mobile-grass-edge.html`.
- **Deeper shadows on the sculpture, same glow.** Shadow information is baked per vertex (`tools/bake_ao_mind.py`, stored in `figure-mind.json`). Crevices (under the jaw, eyes, ears, armpits, chest lines) now darken strongly and the side away from the light is darker, while the rim glow and halo stay at full strength. Each head layer only shadows itself, so there are no dark bands between the layers when they lift. Tuning: `SHADOW_DEPTH`. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-deeper-shadows.html`.
- **Sculpture looks the same in every mode.** It is lit by its own fixed key light (the day sun's direction) instead of each mode's sun or moon. With the deeper shadows, the low sunrise behind it in Ocean, and the moon off to the side, had been showing mostly its shadow side, so it looked dimmer. Sky, water and grass keep their own lighting. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-same-glow-all-modes.html`.
- **Interface highlight is always white.** Moon mode used a lime accent (#D8F27A) for the selected mode button, the menu hover and the About panel's label and link. These are now white, like the other modes. The fireflies keep their green glow. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-white-accent.html`.
- **About makes every swarm circle the sculpture.** What the fireflies already did at night now happens in all three modes. While About is open, all the dragonflies (sun, extras included) and the glass blobs (ocean, above the waterline) leave the cursor and slowly orbit the sculpture, at the same speed and radius as the fireflies. They return to normal when About closes. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-about-orbit.html`.
- **UI glass = clear glass (option B).** Three directions were compared side by side in `prototypes/ui-options.html` (A frosted, B realistic glass, C always-accessible solid; the variants live in `v2-ui-options.html?ui=…`). Juan chose B, tuned with only a light blur (~3–4 px). The toggle, menu and About panel show the scene lightly blurred through a lens that bends at the bevel, with a colour fringe at the edge, a bright top glare, a crisp rim and a soft shadow. Text is white with a soft shadow in every mode, so the About panel's dark-green day text is gone. Every unselected mode button now has a hover state, like the menu items. Previous glass kept in `v2-alt-before-clear-glass.html`.
- **Glass a little more frosted, the About panel most of all.** Each glass element has its own blur radius: 5 px on the toggle and menu, 11 px on the About panel (the last value in `GLASS`). The blur pattern is randomly rotated per pixel, so bright points behind (fireflies, glints) read as soft frosted glows. The tint is slightly lighter by sun and ocean.
- **Text: dark only on the frosted About panel.** The About panel uses near-black text (#111316) by sun and ocean and white at night, with no shadow. Everything else (brand, top-right lines, hint, both menus) stays white with a soft shadow. Near-black was tried everywhere and was clearly less readable over the mid-tone sky, water and grass, and through the thinner menu glass. Dark-everywhere version: not kept as a file; `v2-alt-before-dark-text.html` is the state before.
- **Menu brackets appear on hover.** At rest the menu reads Lab · Work · Art · About; on hover or keyboard focus the brand brackets fade in and slide outward to sit tight around the word, "(Work)" (0.38 s ease-out), without moving anything else. Screen readers hear the plain words. With reduced motion they only fade. The parenthetical voice stays everywhere else (meta lines, hint, toast). Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-menu-brackets.html`.
- **Edge to edge on phones.** The scene now fills the whole screen, underneath the status bar and the browser's toolbars (`viewport-fit=cover`, canvas at the large viewport height), so Safari's translucent bars show the scene instead of a flat colour. The brand, toggle, menu and hint stay inside the safe areas (notch, home bar). Canvas size, pointer mapping and glass placement now follow the canvas itself, not the window. The fallback page colour matches each mode (blue sun, near-black moon, dusky violet ocean). Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-edge-to-edge.html`. [Needs a real-iPhone check]
- **Leaner chrome.** Removed the top-right lines "(Senior Visual Designer)" and "(From Mexico, based in San Francisco)", the "(Move) (Drag)" hint and the prototype note. The subtitle now reads "Designing what's next, and building it. Visual design, 3D and code, grounded in people." Juangarcia(mx) is bigger: 34 px on desktop (was 22), 28 px on phones. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-copy-trim.html`.
- **UI = Clear · dimmed glass, with microinteractions.** Chosen from the round-4 options in `prototypes/ui-options.html` (rounds kept: `ui-options-round1.html`; variants live in `v2-ui-options.html?ui=…`). The glass has no tint and about 2.4× the earlier blur (toggle and menu 12 px, About panel 26 px), lets through about 30% less light, and its glare and rim are at about 35%, so it never outshines what's on it. All text and icons on the glass are white in every mode, the About panel included; this replaces the dark About text by sun and ocean. Microinteractions from the plain-white option, restyled for the glass: a white thumb springs between sun, moon and ocean (selected icon in ink); a soft white pill glides to the hovered menu item while the brackets slide in; buttons press in; the mode icons nudge on hover; the About text reveals line by line; the coffee arrow nudges. With reduced motion everything simply fades. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-clear-dimmed.html`.
- **Softer glow:** the sculpture glows 15% less at rest and gets a gentler hover boost (+45% instead of +60%), the same in every mode and on every device. The face and the head layers now read through the glow. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-closer-softer-glow.html`.
- **Ocean rocks.** The rock terrain from `portfolio 3d2.blend` is baked into a heightmap (`assets/data/rocks.json`) and rendered as rock stacks around the ocean. Its layout is kept but cleared from a 21–32 m lagoon around the sculpture, because Blender's layout put rocks inside the camera's orbit. The rocks grow taller toward the horizon (`ROCK_HEIGHT`) and leave an open gap toward the sunrise, so the sun and its glitter path stay clear. Warm, dark rock, wet near the waterline, a rim light from the low sun, and haze with distance. The water turns shallow and turquoise over submerged rock and foams where rock meets the surface. Rocks appear in the ocean mode only.
- **Cinema print texture on the environment.** The 3D scene gets a film-print finish: a warm halation glow around bright light (the sun and the sculpture's edge), a deeper lens vignette, a faint colour split toward the corners, fine grain and a barely-there flicker. It is applied before the glass, so text, buttons and the glass stay clean. The grain changes **3 times a second and slowly dissolves** from one pattern into the next (`GRAIN_RATE`); the original 24/s felt too busy, and a 24/s or 12/s dissolve was also compared. With reduced motion the grain holds still. Three directions were compared in `prototypes/grain-options-round1.html` (A 35 mm film, B cotton paper, C cinema print) and the speeds in `prototypes/grain-options.html` (sandbox: `v2-grain-options.html?grain=…&gs=…`). Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-grain.html`.

- **Scroll story is the landing** (`v2-sun-moon-field.html` and `index.html`; `v2-scroll-story.html` is the same page): scrolling moves the camera through four snapped chapters. (0) the field, (1) About (the existing face focus, now scrubbed by scroll), (2) Open mind (camera rises to the other 3/4 and looks down into the skull while the heads float well clear, 55 cm / 1.25 m), (3) Explore (pulls back face-on to the whole sculpture, with Lab / Work / Art below). Scrolling is free (no forced steps): chapters are 1.7 screens of scrolling apart so the camera drifts slowly, panels only appear as you near a chapter point, and when you stop scrolling within reach of one a gentle magnetic pull eases you onto it (`MAGNET`). The pull is fairly firm (reaches about a third of a chapter, settles in well under a second) and one-way: it only pulls toward a chapter you're scrolling toward, never back to one you're leaving (apart from a tiny overshoot). Softer two-way magnet kept in `v2-scroll-story-alt-soft-magnet.html`. The interface fades out after the field. Snapped-steps version (rejected: felt forced) kept in `v2-scroll-story-alt-snapped.html`. Clicking the sculpture or (About) scrolls to chapter 1; Esc or (Close) goes back to the top. Copy for chapters 2–3 and the "(Scroll)" cue are placeholders pending Juan's OK. The panels use the clear, dimmed glass and its staggered text reveal, which now plays as each chapter arrives instead of after the About click. Pre-scroll landing kept in `v2-alt-before-scroll-story.html`.
- **Stacked sculpture shadows (2×).** The baked shadow is now applied twice, like duplicating a multiply layer (`SHADOW_LAYERS`, default 2; compare with `?shadow=1`, `?shadow=2`, `?shadow=3`). The deepest point was already near black, but the body is lit so brightly that half-shadowed areas still clipped to white; stacking mainly deepens those mid-tones, so the jaw, eyes, ears and chest lines read more clearly. The rim glow and halo keep their previous shadow amount, so the glow is unchanged and open, lit surfaces stay the same. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-stacked-shadows.html`.
- **About keeps the hover glow.** When the sculpture comes closer for About, it fades up to its hover glow (+45%, plus the matching light on the grass) instead of dimming by 38%. Closer now means brighter. Hover still switches off in About; the glow comes from the About state itself. Previous version kept in `v2-alt-before-about-glow.html`.

## Rejected
- Inflatable playground as *the* concept (kept only as an idea). Real-time soft-body physics. 3D scan of Juan.
- Volumetric clouds; footprint mask clearing ring; stencil "grass never over sculpture".
- LiquidGlass library ([ybouane/liquidglass](https://github.com/ybouane/liquidglass)) for the UI glass: tried on the toggle, the menu and the About panel; Juan preferred the built-in glass. It also cost about 20% of the frame rate.
- Night (smoked) glass in every mode, with stronger smoke by sun and ocean and white panel text: Juan preferred the clear glass per mode.
- Raised camera (eye ~4.6 m looking down into the open head, desktop 11 m out): too high, exposed the rough areas close to the sculpture. Dark creases between the head layers: didn't make sense on a glowing material.
- 35 mm film grain and cotton-paper texture for the environment: Juan chose the cinema print. Paper also hid the sculpture's face at first strength.
